# CCSU Multiobserver — Canonical Correction v1.1

**Status:** prospective, post-trial addendum  
**Adopted:** 2026-07-26  
**Scope:** terminology, P1 interpretation, and prospective inferential rules  
**Non-retroactivity:** CCSU-MO-PREREG-001 v1.0, its hashes, trajectories, analyses, and decisions remain unchanged.

## 1. Scientific scope

The multiobserver extension is intended for a delimited scientific niche, including interdisciplinary configurations such as Cosmology, Physics, Mathematics, and Philosophy. Every application must freeze:

\[
\mathcal D=(\text{niche},\text{problem},\text{scale},\text{observers},\text{Atlas},\text{translations},\text{observables}).
\]

The Atlas coordinates auditable relations among local models. It is not an omniscient metaobserver and does not impose a single ontology.

## 2. Canonical terminology

The expression **“absolute public convergence”** is retired.

The canonical term is **“domain-conditioned public coherence”**: auditable compatibility of translatable relations inside a frozen \(\mathcal D\). It does not require consensus, zero dispersion, or universal agreement.

When \(r_Q=D_Q^{final}/D_Q^{initial}<1\), the narrower diagnostic term is **“intradomain public-disagreement contraction.”**

The distinctions are:

- **coherence:** compatible relations across translations;
- **contraction:** a temporal reduction of public disagreement;
- **consensus:** an optional local outcome, never a CCSU requirement;
- **plurality:** local models and private dimensions remain non-identical.

## 3. P1, prospectively

**P1 — Domain-conditioned public coherence.** Within a frozen scientific niche, HYBRID should coordinate translatable relations better than IND without erasing local models.

Primary comparison:

\[
r_Q^{HYBRID}<r_Q^{IND}.
\]

Separate diagnostic:

\[
r_Q^{HYBRID}<1.
\]

The second inequality indicates intradomain contraction only. Neither inequality establishes absolute or universal convergence. Private-retention criteria remain required.

## 4. P3 inferential rule, prospectively

For future confirmatory runs:

1. \(\Delta recall\) is the P3 efficacy estimand and supplies the single primary P3 p-value.
2. FPR is a separate safety/non-inferiority gate.
3. The FPR gate passes only when its one-sided 95% upper confidence bound is at most 0.05.
4. The FPR p-value does not replace the efficacy p-value and does not enter the Holm family.
5. The Holm family contains one efficacy p-value for each of P1–P4.

This resolves the ambiguity exposed by v1.0, in which the FPR point estimate was below 0.05 but its uncertainty crossed the threshold.

## 5. P5 control rule, prospectively

P5 remains a positive control rather than a substantive CCSU prediction. If P5 fails, the run receives:

**CONTROL INVALID / INTERPRETATION SUSPENDED**

P1–P4 results remain recorded, but they are not treated as five equivalent refutations and cannot support a conclusive canonical interpretation until the control failure is explained in a separately registered run.

## 6. Historical boundary

These rules govern only protocols that cite Canonical Correction v1.1 before simulation. They do not reclassify the completed v1.0 run.
